# imagegen-service — a free, private image maker for your own computer

**Type a description, get a picture.** imagegen-service turns words like *"a stone bridge over a
misty gorge"* into an image — and it does the whole job on **your own computer**, using your
computer's graphics card. Nothing is sent to a company's servers, there's no monthly subscription,
and there's no per-picture fee. Once it's set up, it's yours.

Here's an example it made — the words *"a stone bridge over a misty gorge"* in the "oil painting"
style:

![An oil-painting-style image of a stone bridge over a misty gorge, generated by the service](images/test-ui-result.png)

## Wait — what actually is this? (read this part)

This is the one thing people get confused about, so let's be clear up front:

**imagegen-service is not a finished app you open and use every day. It's a building block.**

Think of it like the engine of a car, not the car. On its own it just sits there, quietly ready.
Its job is to be the thing **other apps call whenever they need a picture** — without those apps
having to pay an online service or send your words to the cloud.

Once it's running on one computer, **any app on your home network can ask it for images.** For
example:

- **A game** that needs to illustrate a scene, a character, or an item on the fly.
- **A writing or storytelling app** that drops in artwork to match what you just wrote.
- **Your own little scripts or projects** — anything you build that wants pictures, for free,
  without an account.

It *does* come with a simple built-in web page for trying it out yourself (type a prompt, see the
picture), which is great for testing and for a quick play. But the real point is to be the free,
private picture-maker sitting behind your other apps. See **[Using it](using-it.md)** for both.

## What you need (the honest version)

To run imagegen-service, the computer needs:

- **Windows or Ubuntu Linux.** (Sorry, **not a Mac** — Macs can't run the graphics part this uses.)
- **A supported NVIDIA graphics card.** This is the important one. Drawing an image is heavy work,
  and it's the **graphics card** (the "GPU") that does it. NVIDIA is the specific brand this is
  built for. No NVIDIA card means this can't run on that machine.

Don't have an NVIDIA graphics card in your main computer? You're not stuck — you can install this
on **another** computer that does have one (an old gaming PC is perfect) and let your apps reach it
over your home network. But the machine doing the drawing needs that NVIDIA card.

Not sure whether you have one? Each install page starts by showing you how to check in a click or
two.

## The art styles

You can generate a plain, realistic picture, or pick one of **12 art styles** to give it a
distinct look. Here they are, each with the feel it gives:

| Style | The feel |
|---|---|
| *(no style)* | Realistic — a normal, photo-like picture |
| pixel art | Retro |
| oil painting | Classic |
| comic book | Bold |
| lego-style | Blocky |
| pencil sketch | Hand-drawn |
| watercolour | Soft |
| anime | Animated |
| storybook | Whimsical |
| 3d | Pixar-ish |
| cyberpunk | Neon |
| ukiyo-e | Japanese |
| claymation | Clay |

You can also just describe a style in your own words (like "noir" or "ghibli") and it'll do its
best, even without a dedicated style file.
